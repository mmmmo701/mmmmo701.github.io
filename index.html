Game Theory Books
I had been wanting to know what algorithmic game theory was forever, and Twenty Lectures in Algorithmic Game Theory by Tim Roughgarden, together with his lecture and lecture notes, are really helpful in learning algorithmic game theory. One unique thing about Roughgarden's books and lecture is that, unlike most courses and books like NRTV that starts with equilibriums, they started with auctions and mechanism design, which is the first area that drives the entire field of algorithmic game theory, before transitioning to topics like price of anarchy and PPAD completeness. I also started reading NRTV and I really liked it so far

Research

I love digging deep into problems. Here are some of my current and past research projects.

Information-Theoretic Evaluation and Design of Locality-Sensitive Hashing for Protein Alignment
Locality Sensitive Hashing (LSH) is a technique of algorithms used to approximate edit distances. This research focuses on constructing a metric to measure the performance of these algorithms.
Conducted under SURA →
Generalizing Tiered Trees to Hypertrees
A tiered tree is a tree with a tiering function. We generalized this concept into hypergraphs and hypertrees and designed and implemented a C library to compute hypertree weights and to extend the Tutte polynomial to hypertrees.
Conducted under PROMYS →
Theoretical Approach on Assessing the Accuracy of the Shortest Path Non-Optimal Algorithm for 2-Dimensional Grids with Obstacles
Traditional search algorithms on 2D grids guarantee to find the shortest path at the cost of quadratic time complexity. Non-optimal algorithms that trade accuracy for speed have emerged to address the issue. This research evaluates the accuracy of two non-optimal algorithms using experimental data and theoretical analysis.
Published at ICACS '24 →

NashHack

A game made by Claude based on my personality. This is a tiny roguelike in the spirit of NetHack, except every fight is a 2×2 zero-sum game: you pick a row, the monster picks a column, and the cell decides who bleeds. Every monster plays a fixed strategy. Most of them can be exploited if you watch what they do, but the Nash Knight can't. Descend five floors and bring the Ackermann down to a base case.
